3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. One "shop clerk" agent with get_lease, prepare_rent_payment and resolve_ticket could do it in about 3 tool calls and maybe 4 model calls. The team used 13 model calls and about 35k tokens. Here's why the team is overhead on this ticket:

  • There is no judgment and no conflicting goals. The amount and the date are right there in leases.
  • The Boss → Facilities → Accounting relay exists only because of the org chart. Each hop re-sends a whole role prompt and tool list.
  • Each agent re-fetches facts the last one already had: get_cash_position ran 3× in ticket 102.
  • The safety doesn't depend on there being several agents. Human approval, the no-negative-cash check, and amounts coming from the DB all live in the MCP tools, so a single agent would be exactly as safe.

Ticket 101 would also work as one agent. It's a straight line: check_stock → get_vendor_status → prepare_invoice_payment → prepare_purchase_order → save_customer_draft. That's about 5 tool calls instead of 22. The split did buy something real, though. Customer Service has no cash tools and a prompt that forbids internal talk, which is why the draft never mentions the overdue bill. One agent that just read the invoice might have leaked it.

Where the team earned its keep was 103: customer happiness (discount), margin (Accounting), stock (Inventory), and cash all pull in different directions, and the Boss had to weigh separate reports. Even there, the real win came from the tool guardrail, not from agents debating.

My takeaway: a team is worth the token cost when there are competing objectives or a need for separation of duties. For rule-following tickets with all the facts in one table, one agent is cheaper, faster, and just as safe.

5 · Three new problems it could not solve, and what I'd add

  1. Taking the Yale AI Club's money, which would have closed ticket 103's $112 gap. The obvious business move on 103 was: "pay a deposit and we'll order the other 12 today". But the system only models cash going out. There's no sales or receivables table, and no tool can record a customer payment.
    • Tools to add: create_customer_invoice, record_customer_payment (writes a receipts table and raises cash_accounts, only after a human confirms the money arrived), and list_receivables.
    • Agent to add: a Sales / Billing agent that owns quotes, deposits and receivables. Plus a rule for Accounting that a deposit must clear before it funds a restock PO.
    • With these, 103 could have become "deposit received → 12-unit PO → all 20 delivered 9/5".
  2. Receiving PO #1 when it shows up on 2026-09-05. The team ordered Tauhid's tee, but nothing can receive it. No tool raises inventory.qty or marks a PO received, and desk.date_today never moves past 8/31. So ticket 101 is "resolved" while S still shows 0 forever, and nobody will tell Tauhid it arrived.
    • Tools to add: receive_purchase_order(po_id, qty_received) (updates inventory and handles short shipments), advance_shop_date, and reopen_ticket / create_followup_ticket.
    • Agent to add: a Receiving / Scheduler agent that wakes up on ETA dates and checks in deliveries. When the goods land, it hands Customer Service a "your order is in" draft.
  3. Paying October rent: $2,400 due 2026-10-02 with $152 in the bank. The Cash tab ends at $152, and leases.next_due is now 10/02. The current team can only discover the problem at the last minute, when prepare_rent_payment refuses for cash. It can't forecast, prioritize ahead of time, or find money.
    • Tools to add: forecast_cash(days), built on scheduled obligations plus expected receipts from #1. Also request_credit_line / draw_credit (human approval, logged like payments) and draft_landlord_message to ask for a short extension.
    • Agent to add: a Finance / Treasury agent that runs a weekly cash forecast and opens its own ticket weeks before a shortfall.
    • Facilities would also need lease-negotiation tools: lease terms, an amendment history, and a human-approved update_lease.
Bottom line: the agents made good routing calls and never touched money a human didn't approve. The cash math on the Cash tab is exact. But the most valuable safety moment (103's refused $264 PO) came from a tool guardrail. Two of the three problems the team can't solve trace back to the same gap: this shop can only spend money, never receive it.
